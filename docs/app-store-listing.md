## Description

`1273 / 4000 characters`

```
Five ways to settle it.

super fair odds is a small collection of randomizers, each one built to feel like the real object rather than a button that prints an answer.


FLIP A COIN
One flip, or best of three. The coin tumbles and lands, and the face only changes at the moment it is edge-on, so a flip reads as a flip.

ROLL DICE
One, two or three dice, thrown as real cubes and totalled where they stop.

DRAW A CARD
A full 52-card deck, drawn without replacement. The stack visibly thins as you go and reshuffles itself once it runs out, or shuffle whenever you like.

SPIN A WHEEL
Write your own options and the wheel becomes exactly that list. It remembers them for next time.

PICK A NUMBER
Any range you like, from 1-10 to anything you type. One number lands, ringed by others drawn from the same range.


EVERY RESULT, KEPT
A history tab records what you got and when, grouped by day and clearable whenever you want.

MADE TO FEEL RIGHT
Weighted motion, a haptic tap on every landing, and no waiting around. Reduced Motion and VoiceOver are both supported.

COMPLETELY PRIVATE
No accounts. No ads. No tracking. No analytics. No network requests of any kind. Nothing leaves your phone, because nothing is ever sent anywhere, and the whole app works in airplane mode.
```

## App Review Notes

`2501 / 4000 characters`

```
DEMO ACCESS
No account, login, or setup of any kind is required. The app opens on a grid of five activities, each one reachable in a single tap, and nothing is gated or hidden behind a purchase. The app makes no network requests at all and runs fully in airplane mode. It requests no system permissions.


NON-OBVIOUS UI - PLEASE READ

The app contains one deliberately undocumented interaction. It is disclosed here in full, per Guideline 2.3.1.

Two invisible 76x76pt touch targets sit in the lower-left and lower-right corners of the stage around the coin, dice, card, wheel or number. Tapping one pre-selects an extreme outcome for the next result:

  left  = the low end  (heads / all ones / red / first wheel option / minimum)
  right = the high end (tails / all sixes / black / last option / maximum)

Feedback is haptic only - one pulse for the low end, two for the high end, and a success notification if the same corner is tapped again to cancel. Nothing appears on screen.

This is a party-trick affordance: the digital equivalent of a two-headed coin, letting the person holding the phone call a result in advance for someone across the table. Specifically, it is:

  - entirely local and user-invoked; nothing is transmitted, recorded or shared
  - not persisted - leaving an activity screen clears any pre-selection
  - unconnected to money, wagering, scoring, purchases or rewards
  - invisible in the UI and excluded from the accessibility tree by design, since a visible control would defeat its purpose

It would be difficult to discover without being told, which is exactly why it is spelled out here rather than left for review to find. The feature is also documented publicly in the open-source repository at github.com/sunmatthew/super-fair-odds, under "Hidden outcomes" in CLAUDE.md.


AGE RATING
There is no gambling or simulated gambling. No wagering, no betting, no virtual currency, no casino imagery, and nothing to win or lose. The dice, cards and wheel are decision-making tools. Simulated Gambling is answered None.


DATA COLLECTION
None. No analytics, no advertising, no third-party SDKs, no tracking, no identifiers. The only stored data is the user's own wheel option list and their result history, both held in local device storage and both clearable from within the app. This matches the "Data Not Collected" nutrition label and the app's privacy manifest.


IN-APP PURCHASES
```
